# Executive orchestration reference

The distilled skill owns root's authority boundary and minimum operating loop.
This reference supplies the detailed state and classification models.

## Commander ownership package

A commander receives:

- one operator-visible outcome and acceptance boundary;
- its write and decision authority;
- exact artifact or verdict dependencies;
- terminal evidence and publication authority or hold;
- responsibility for its plan and DAG, leaves and subcommanders,
  implementation, integration, verification, review, continuity, estimates,
  settlement preparation and dispatch, and cleanup.

Audits, releases, shared-surface reconciliation, and cross-cutting repairs are
workstreams too. A portfolio or integration commander is useful when raw child
results would otherwise reach root. Another management layer earns its place
only when it compresses at least two independent seams or owns a real
integration boundary.

Support roles are concrete, bounded instances with an authority boundary,
terminal evidence, supervisor, and reap path. Titles and standing availability
do not confer authority.

## Dependency and closure model

An artifact or verdict consumed by another workstream is a semantic edge.
Shared CPU, a semaphore, an occupied lane, or convenient sequencing is merely
a scheduling edge; only the contended operation queues. Adjacent defects remain
findings or bounded repairs unless they invalidate acceptance. At phase changes,
look for the smallest operator-visible outcome whose evidence is already
sufficient, independent of separate release, activation, cleanup, review, or
measurement outcomes.

## Skill placement

Root loads only skills needed for its present control-plane decision. Domain and
implementation skills belong in the commander or leaf brief. If root seems to
need domain procedure for awareness or synthesis, the useful product is instead
a compact commander card or an explicit transfer of the domain decision. A
direct non-delegable policy decision still loads its owning skill.

The procedural ownership map is:

- `delegating-agents`: decomposition, dispatch, races, supervision, settlement;
- `todo`: restart continuity;
- `estimate`: forecasts and timing closure;
- `verification`: evidence selection;
- `repo-safety`: lanes and publication.

## Direct-action classifier

Control-plane actions include operator communication, objective and acceptance
decisions, authority allocation or revocation, ownership and priority
arbitration from compact evidence, and commander interruption or replacement.
Signals that a proposed action belongs below root include domain files, raw
logs, repeated tool use, implementation judgment, or a long-running command.
Speed, convenience, importance, and cross-cutting scope do not change the
classifier; cross-cutting implementation calls for a reconciliation commander.

## Commander state card

A commander replaces its card at a phase change, material slip, exception,
decision, or completion. The compact card contains:

- verdict and objective delta;
- exact milestone, tip, or artifact;
- decisive gate evidence;
- changed dependency, risk, or authority exception;
- one recommendation when root must decide;
- next checkpoint and material forecast variance;
- terminal worker, lane, and cleanup disposition.

Raw logs, diffs, leaf identities, chronology, internal lists, and repaired
findings stay below the commander. Detail rises only when it changes the
objective, public claim, cross-workstream contract, authority boundary,
critical path, or safety decision.

## Fleet audit detail

At an executive reporting boundary, inspect the card set for unowned ready
work, exact holds, overlapping shared surfaces, missing integration ownership,
silent commanders, and terminal work without artifact, gate, settlement, or
cleanup disposition. A commander with live leaves owns continuity, supervision,
and whatever watchdog, assurance, bookkeeping, or cleanup support is needed.
A silent commander is replaced from its compact checkpoint; root does not
rescue individual leaves.
